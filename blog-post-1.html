COMMUNITY & YOUTH EMPOWERMENT

Helping Our Children Thrive: The Power of Positive Youth Development

Tiffany Tucker
Founder & Managing Principal, MyMark Advisory LLC
October 2024 • 6 min read

Every day we wake up to start our day, with our family in mind.

As a parent myself, I am well aware of the challenges we face raising our children in New York City. My recommendation to help our children thrive academically, socially, emotionally, and physically, is to keep them engaged in positive youth development programs.

This means we will have to sit with our children, find out their likes and dislikes, and then find activities that match their interests. Data strongly underscores the importance of this step: research highlighted by the Office of Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention (OJJDP) shows that roughly 35% of 12-year-olds are left unsupervised regularly during working hours, putting them at a much higher risk for negative influences.

Statistics clearly prove the immense value of keeping young people engaged in structured, positive environments:

>50%
Substance Abuse Prevention
Evidence-based youth prevention programs can dramatically lower substance abuse rates, reducing illicit drug use by over 50% (Addiction Policy Forum).
90%
Graduation Success
CASEL meta-analyses confirm improved attendance and conduct, with intensive initiatives achieving up to a 90% graduation rate vs. 60% for non-participants.
2×
College Completion
Alumni tracking published in BMC Public Health found participants were twice as likely to finish college and achieve long-term financial stability.

Character, Values, and Beyond the Work Ethic

We are hard-working people and I commend us for our work ethics. But the real value is in the time we make to sit and listen to our children and impart good character traits in them, not just show them we are physically strong and can work long hours. That is good too, but it can only take them so far.

“What type of person do you want your children to become? For me, I pray for them to be kind, spiritually grounded, able to cope with all of life’s ups and downs, and that they will treat people with respect because they respect themselves. If they use their gifts and talents to become a celebrity or a firefighter, I will be happy as long as they are happy, safe, and living with integrity.”
